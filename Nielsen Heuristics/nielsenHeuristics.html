I believe that the heuristic that most relates to Norman’s chapter 1 is “2: Match Between the System and the Real World”. In chapter 1, Norman argues that bad design, not user error, is always at fault when it comes to bad designs. A big aspect of this concept and maybe a reason why some designs won’t work is this rule. Designs that use concepts from the real world do a much better job at communicating its purpose to the user instantly. An example of this might be water dispensers.Most water dispensers will give you the option to dispense hot and cold water. To display this, they will usually have a red dispenser for the hot water and a blue dispenser for the cold dispenser. Another option they might have is a red fire symbol and a blue snowflake symbol for its respective temperature.This design choice works because humans understand fire and the color red as hot as well as snow and the color blue as cold. This immediately communicates the message across and wouldn’t be as effective if the icons were replaced with something else.

The two readings overlap because they both argue that the user shouldn’t have to learn a system from scratch, and should immediately understand the idea. They both argue that the burden shouldn’t be placed on the user to understand.
