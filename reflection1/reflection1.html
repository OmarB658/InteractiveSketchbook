I agree with Norman in the fact that when a user looks at a specific design, they should immediately be able to tell what the purpose of it is. If the user is confused at all, it’s on the developer, not the user.

I believe without the instructions in my micro project, it's a little more difficult to figure out what needs to be done. With the instructions, this provides a clear signifier on what to do, i.e. typing in your desired betting amount on the betting screens. Since my micro sketch has all the instructions laid out through each step, I believe the affordances are clear and concise. With the exception of what the goal is, I didn’t include anywhere what the whole goal of the game is, which means it might be confusing what the user needs to do until they go through the entire step process.

The feedback is immediate and legible. Everytime you click on the screen, one or both of the circles will grow or shrink, which immediately shows that something is happening. However, there is a chance that if the user clicks, none of the circles will grow at all, which means the system might have failed to communicate a new state clearly in this instance. I believe Norman would likely criticize this aspect of the micro sketch as a failure to communicate properly. I have the percentage chances of how likely it is for each circle to grow, however they’re tucked away on the side and might not be super visible to someone not paying attention.

As mentioned before, the feedback is fairly clear most of the time. The user will clearly see the circles growing and shrinking and understand what’s happening. The user is able to see how the states of the two circles change over time the more they click.

The biggest constraint in my system is that the user is only able to click and has no way of controlling the sizes of the circles beyond that. No matter what they do, one of the circles will always have a much higher chance of growing larger while the other doesn’t.

I believe my micro sketch’s biggest design weakness is how little control the user actually has and that it isn’t immediately clear why the user is betting these numbers at the beginning to begin with. This might confuse the user initially.

My biggest takeaway from the text is that interaction design is about making sure the user understands what the purpose of a specific design is almost immediately. A weak design might confuse the user or guide them into an unintended path. I agree with Norman’s idea that designers need to assume that users will make mistakes and you need to design systems that are forgiving and understanding.

I enjoyed the reading, because it makes me think in a slightly different way when designing different softwares. It made me slightly more aware of how users interact with a specific design. I want to try and think about affirmation, feedback, mapping, and constraints next time I design a system.
